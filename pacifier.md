## Overview
The Sonic Pacifier (SP-4) is a handheld acoustic compliance emitter with an integrated probe module, certified on Earth in 2124 and carried aboard the colony vessel at the October 9, 2125 departure. It is the only weapon pattern the [MRE]({{ '/mre.html' | relative_url }}) will fabricate, and it will do so only for the Chief of Security, upon live biometric confirmation. The device synthesizes tightly directional audible sound from ultrasonic carriers for hailing, warning, and short-range pain compliance; its separately gated probe module provides neuromuscular incapacitation for the rare cases in which acoustics are ineffective or infeasible. The pattern has not been revised since departure.

## Origin & Development
Pre-Departure Certification: The SP-4 was designed and certified on Earth as the mission's sole authorized force instrument, intended to cover the full escalation ladder — hail, warn, compel, incapacitate — in a single tool small enough for a colony with no standing police force. Marks 1 through 3 were Earth-side prototypes; the SP-4 pattern was frozen at certification and has never been amended.
The Single-Pattern Rule: The MRE refuses all other weapon patterns. No firearm, munition, or directed-energy design exists in its library, and the pacifier pattern is released to no citizen other than the Chief of Security. Fabrication requires the Chief's live biometric confirmation; probe cartridges are printed singly, under the same gate, and entered in the public ledger.
Carry Doctrine: Emitter-down carry, hail-first engagement, and Council review of every discharge were written into the mission charter before departure and remain in force. The Chief of Security is the only sworn officer authorized to carry the device.

## Engineering & Pattern Governance
Emitter Array: A 120 mm aperture of 1,600 PMUT elements forms ultrasonic carriers that demodulate in air into a narrow audible beam; the array face is unobstructed, with probe bores flanking it.
Probe Module: A nitrogen-cartridge launcher fires two tethered barbed probes from bores beside the array. Shaped sub-joule pulses produce neuromuscular incapacitation for a standard five-second cycle. The module is armed only by live biometric confirmation, separate from the acoustic modes, and each cycle is individually authorized and logged.
Power & Logging: A shared solid-state cell serves the array and the module's capacitor bank. Every activation, mode change, and discharge is written to the public ledger.
Fabrication: The MRE holds the frozen pattern. No colonial fab lab is licensed to produce the device or its cartridges; spare cartridges are printed on demand under the Chief's biometric gate and metered by the ledger.

## Operating Modes & Physiological Effects
The SP-4 provides five settings: Hail (directional voice), Warning (wide-beam alert tones), Lullaby (acoustic compliance), Beacon (rescue and wildlife deterrence), and Probe (neuromuscular incapacitation). The colonial name derives from the Lullaby setting; the device is universally called "the pacifier," and "the binky" among the colony's youth.
In Lullaby mode, the demodulated 1–5 kHz band produces aversive pain, nausea, vertigo, and tinnitus, with typical recovery under two hours and no lasting effect. It does not render unconsciousness — "stun" is a colonial misnomer for this mode. Ordinary foam earplugs reduce it to mere aversion, and active noise-canceling helmets defeat it entirely; this defeatability is a charter requirement, not an oversight.
Probe mode is the device's only true incapacitant, and its only less-lethal — rather than non-lethal — function. Serious injury is rare but documented, chiefly from falls during incapacitation; officers are trained to break falls, and barbed-probe removal is a clinic procedure. Every probe discharge triggers mandatory medical follow-up and Council review. The module exists for the cases acoustics cannot reach: a protected or determined subject, a deaf subject, or thin air. It has never been discharged on Ares.

## Societal & Political Impact
A. The Single Pattern
The MRE's refusal of every other weapon pattern is the colony's quietest and most absolute law: its entire monopoly on force is one frozen file, released to one person, biometrically. Colonists grow up knowing that the only weapon on the moon is a museum piece the replicator keeps faithfully reprinting — and that its worst setting has never been used.
B. Everyday Dual Use
The same unit that ends a confrontation shoos fauna off landing pads, broadcasts storm warnings upriver, and serves as a rescue beacon. Most colonists have heard a pacifier only at a distance, announcing weather.
The device is not without critics. The phrase "a pain ray with a nursery name" remains in circulation among the colony's small abolitionist press, and the unused probe module is its favorite subject: a thing carried for a moment that has never come, and which some argue should never have been packed. Council reviews of the pattern are public. None has ever amended it.

## Technical Evolution & The Frozen Pattern
There is no SP-5. The certification board that froze the pattern dissolved on Earth generations ago, and the MRE maintains the file without deviation. Any amendment would require Council action and full pattern re-certification — a process that has been petitioned eleven times and completed never. The device carried by the Chief of Security today is functionally identical to the one that left Earth on October 9, 2125, down to the original cartridge design, replaced quarterly and never fired.

## Historical Impact
The SP-4's significance lies less in what it does than in what the colony required it not to do: it cannot be printed for anyone but the Chief, cannot discharge unlogged, cannot be turned on an assembly, and can be defeated by any citizen with a pair of earplugs. Historians cite the single-pattern rule as the colony's clearest statement that security is a service, not a posture — and its critics cite the unused probe module as proof that even a utopia packs for the world it fears.
