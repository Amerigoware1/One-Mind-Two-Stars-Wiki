## Overview
The Papilio-Class Personal Flight Harness, colloquially known as "Butterflies," is a strap-on, human-piloted mobility unit designed for canyon descent, cliff ascent, and tight-space exploration on Ares. Rejecting the limitations of remote micro-drones, the Butterfly places the explorer directly in the environment. It is an essential tool for the Global Planetary Survey Team, allowing crew members to safely navigate the treacherous, unmapped verticality of Ares' deep-crust cave systems and gorge networks while maintaining the physical presence required for Galinstanian interaction and lithic translation.
</div>

## Structural Architecture & Propulsion
The unit is built for high output in Ares' 0.8 atm atmosphere without sacrificing compactness or safety.
* **Quad-Rotor EDF Frame:** A rigid, lightweight carbon-fiber and titanium chassis mounts four high-output brushless electric ducted fans (EDFs). The blades are encased in high-impact ducting to maximize thrust efficiency and protect the pilot's limbs and nearby cave flora/structures in tight spaces.
* **Coaxial Contra-Rotating Rotors:** Instead of a wide-span quad-rotor setup that would clip canyon walls, the Butterfly uses stacked coaxial pairs (two blades per arm spinning in opposite directions). This doubles the thrust footprint within a compact backpack width, providing the high bite needed in thin air without cumbersome prop guards.
* **Morphing Smart-Blades:** Utilizing piezoelectric composites and shape-memory alloys, the rotor blades dynamically alter their pitch and camber in real-time. When fighting thin air or high-pressure drop-offs, the blades automatically flex to maximize lift efficiency at higher RPMs.
* **Backpack Chassis:** The central harness distributes weight across the hips and shoulders like a heavy-duty mountaineering pack. A rigid lumbar plate prevents spinal strain during sudden acceleration or deceleration, as the thrust vector pushes directly against the pilot's back.

## Flight Dynamics & Body Control
The Butterfly is designed to make high-speed, three-dimensional maneuvering feel intuitive, almost like swimming through the air.
* **The Harness Tug & Transition:** The system uses a full-body webbed harness with heavy-duty leg loops. On take-off, the lower two rotors spin up first, lifting the hips and tail, while the upper rotors stabilize the chest. This pivots the pilot effortlessly around their center of gravity from a standing ground stance into a face-down, prone flight position within a split second.
* **Prone Aerodynamics:** Flying head-first dramatically reduces wind resistance compared to standing upright, allowing the four rotors to push the pilot forward with remarkable efficiency while providing an incredible, unobstructed field of view for exploration.
* **Physical Steering Integration:** While the flight computer handles vector stabilization, the pilot's physical body acts as an active control surface. Leaning the torso or shifting the hips subtly aids the rotors in banking turns, allowing for fluid, high-speed maneuvers through narrow canyon passages.

## Navigation & Control Mechanics
Canyons on Ares are notorious for unpredictable thermal drafts, wind shears, and downdrafts. The Butterfly's control scheme is designed to prevent cognitive overload during sudden gusts.
* **Gaze-Directed Navigation (The Two-Second Rule):** A high-framerate, inward-facing IR camera tracks the pilot's eye movement or helmet orientation inside a lightweight flight visor. Staring at an outcrop, ridge, or cave opening for more than two seconds locks that coordinate as a waypoint. 
* **The Soft Lock:** To prevent accidental "squirrel-watching" from sending a pilot hurtling into a ravine, the system requires a slight initial body lean or a thumb-trigger press on the hand controller to engage the gaze-lock. The onboard flight computer then instantly dumps maximum power into the windward rotors to hold the vector against canyon turbulence.
* **Corded Hand Controllers:** For absolute reliability in rugged terrain (where wireless signals can lag or drop), physical cords tether two handheld grips to the main backpack frame. Pulling or squeezing the cords dynamically alters throttle (speed), while twisting or tilting the grips manages pitch and altitude.

## Safety & Landing Dynamics
Safety is hard-coded into the Butterfly's flight logic, ensuring that even in the event of pilot panic, the system defaults to survival.
* **Emergency Pitch-Up (Braking):** To stop fast, the control logic reverses the launch sequence. Squeezing both hand triggers simultaneously cuts forward momentum and instantly throttles up the top rotors while dropping the rear thrust. This brings the pilot's legs back down beneath them, transitioning them out of prone flight and into a vertical orientation.
* **Ground Clearance on Touchdown:** As the pilot descends to land, the rear rotors soften their thrust last. This allows the legs to drop into a natural running stance, enabling the pilot to take a couple of steps to safely absorb the remaining kinetic momentum.
* **Redundant Safety Features:** The unit features independent power cells for each rotor pair, automatic terrain-avoidance proximity sensors, and a fail-safe hover-lock that triggers if the hand controllers are dropped entirely.

## Relationship to the Crew
The Butterflies are the literal and figurative vehicles that allow the Book 7–9 expedition to access the deep-crust Memory Veins.
* **Samir** is the Chief Maintenance Officer for the fleet, meticulously logging every flight hour and morphing-blade calibration. 
* **Aulii** uses the Butterfly for precise, silent descents into cave mouths, appreciating how the prone position and gaze-lock allow her to approach Galinstanian archives with the absolute stillness required for lithic translation.
* **Xara** utilizes the unit to reach high vantage points on canyon walls, mapping the macro-geometry of the bismuth network from angles impossible to achieve on foot.
* **The Children (Sora, Nia, Jasper, Jasmine):** Under strict supervision, the older children train on junior, low-altitude, tethered versions of the harness, learning to read the wind and trust their physical steering integration long before they are certified for solo canyon drops.

## Trivia
* The colloquial name "Butterflies" comes from the way the harness pivots the user into a prone, gliding position, combined with the soft, rhythmic hum of the morphing smart-blades adjusting to the wind.
* Sora once tried to "race" a thermal updraft in a narrow gorge and triggered the Emergency Pitch-Up braking sequence three times in one minute. Samir responded by temporarily locking Sora's throttle to 40% until he completed a full module on canyon turbulence dynamics.
* Aulii refuses to use the gaze-directed navigation when approaching a sensitive Memory Vein, preferring to fly manually via the corded grips to ensure her own focus and intention remain entirely her own, unmediated by the flight computer.
* The rigid lumbar plate of the backpack chassis is custom-molded for each crew member. Jasper’s is uniquely reinforced to accommodate the subtle, constant micro-adjustments his dual-channel perception makes while flying.
* The corded hand controllers are braided with a thin strand of bismuth thread, a symbolic and functional nod to the Stewardship Program, ensuring the tethers are not only physically strong but mildly resonance-dampening.
