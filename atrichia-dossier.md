# Dermatological & Social Management

Congenital Atrichia Universalis is a rare genetic condition characterized by the complete absence of hair follicles. In the Niamh-Ciara system, it is understood and managed as a natural variation of human appearance rather than a disability.

</div>

<div class="card-bg" markdown="1">

## Condition Summary

**Diagnosis:**

Congenital Atrichia Universalis

**Etiology:**

Genetic (autosomal-recessive) – no relationship to radiation or acquired alopecia.

**Primary Impact:**

Complete absence of hair follicles on all body surfaces. No eyebrows, eyelashes, or scalp hair.

**Clinical Characteristics:**

The skin is generally smooth and may appear reflective, particularly on the scalp. The absence of hair can affect thermoregulation and removes some of the natural protection normally provided by scalp hair and eyelashes.

The condition is congenital, non-scarring, and non-progressive. It does not require treatment to restore hair growth.

</div>

<div class="card-bg" markdown="1">

## Medical & Lifestyle Considerations

### 🛡️ Protective Measures

* **Thermoregulation:** Hair assists in insulating the scalp; its absence increases heat loss. Lightweight hats, scarves, or other head coverings provide additional warmth in cold environments.
* **UV Protection:** The scalp and exposed skin require protection from ultraviolet radiation. Broad-spectrum SPF 50+ and wide-brimmed headwear are recommended outdoors.
* **Eye Protection:** The absence of eyelashes increases exposure to airborne debris and can contribute to eye dryness. Wraparound sunglasses or lubricating eye drops may be useful as needed.
* **Skin Care:** Gentle, fragrance-free moisturizers help maintain skin hydration. Harsh exfoliants and irritating products are best avoided.

## Social & Identity Expression

### 🎭 Cultural Perspective

* **Community Attitude:** In New Eden, atrichia is viewed as a neutral physical trait, akin to freckles or heterochromia. Visible differences are generally accepted without stigma.
* **Personal Expression:** Individuals may choose to embrace their natural appearance or experiment with wigs, scarves, decorative bands, oils, and other accessories. These choices are matters of personal identity and preference rather than attempts to conceal a medical condition.

### Individual Experiences

**Sam Calder**

Sam uses wigs, oils, metallic bands, and scarves to express gender and mood – not to "hide" the condition but to celebrate its versatility. Their openness has helped normalize visible differences throughout the New Eden community.

Sam's personal philosophy is perhaps best expressed in their own words:

"My head isn't bare – it's a canvas."

**Indigo Keahi-Calder**

Indigo, daughter of Sam Calder and Maya Keahi, represents a second generation living with congenital atrichia. Growing up with a parent who shares the condition provides her with an immediate family perspective on something that might otherwise be considered unusual.

Her individual approach to appearance and self-expression is her own, rather than necessarily following Sam's preferences.

## 📋 Ongoing Care

Annual dermatological examinations are recommended. Although skin-cancer risk is not elevated by the condition itself, dryness and actinic keratosis are monitored as part of routine care.

No medical treatment is required. The condition is stable and primarily affects appearance and certain aspects of skin protection and thermoregulation.

Sam uses a gentle, fragrance-free moisturizer on the scalp and avoids harsh exfoliants. Similar skin-care practices may be adopted by other individuals according to their needs and preferences.
