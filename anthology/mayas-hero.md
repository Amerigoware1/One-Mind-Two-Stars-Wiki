Celectra approached the waiting cave entrance with both bodies moving in easy synchronization. She’d come alone today, planning a quiet soak in the geothermal pool—knees still aching from the sustained seiza yesterday. No council questions, no diplomatic pressure, just general conversation—or silence.

When the door swooshed open at her approach, she felt the cave exhaling cool mineral air, a relief after the morning’s heat.

“Celectra\! Wait up\!”

She turned—both bodies pivoting in unison—to find Maya scrambling up the trail, a large portfolio case strapped across her back and her dark hair escaping from its braid in wind-tossed spirals.

“Maya?” Celectra blinked in surprise. “What are you doing out here?”

“Coming to see you.” The thirteen-year-old girl reached the entrance, breathing hard but grinning. “And the Galinstanians. I brought something.”

She swung the portfolio case around and unzipped it with careful reverence, pulling out a sheaf of large sketch papers. Even upside-down, Celectra—an artist herself—could see the quality of the artwork: confident lines, careful shading, and attention to detail that went beyond simple representation.

Maya spread three drawings on a flat rock near the entrance:

The first showed Stan in his mercury-elegant form, captured mid-flow through a stone chamber. She’d somehow conveyed both solidity and liquidity simultaneously, the way light reflected off his surface while suggesting the deeper consciousness within.

The second depicted a cluster of smaller Galinstanians arranged in what looked like conversation, their forms slightly overlapping in a visual representation of shared thought.

The third was the bismuth network itself—not the physical veins of metal in stone, but the *idea* of it: connections branching and flowing like neurons, like rivers, like the space between thoughts.

“Maya.” Both Celectra’s voices came out soft with wonder. “These are incredible.”

The girl’s whole face lit up. “Really? I’ve been working on them for weeks. I wanted to—” She hesitated, suddenly shy. “I wanted to make something for the cultural exchange program. Lira said you were helping establish formal documentation methods, and I thought maybe art could be part of that? Visual records alongside your telepathic translations?”

The Engineer: *Actually a sound proposal. Visual documentation would supplement verbal records.*

The Weaver: *And it gives Maya legitimate involvement without overwhelming her. Change of plans.*

The Diplomat: *She’s trying so hard to impress you. Be kind. You can soak another time.*

The Wildcard: *She’s trying to contribute something real. That deserves respect, not condescension. You can invite her to soak with you.*

“Come inside,” Celectra said, gathering up the drawings carefully. “Let’s ask Stan directly. If he’s interested, you can sketch him in person—no more working from secondhand descriptions and a distant memory.”

Maya’s expression transformed from hopeful to ecstatic so quickly that Celectra had to suppress a smile. The hero-worship was obvious, almost painfully transparent in the way the younger girl’s eyes tracked her every movement. But unlike Jaxon’s jealousy or the Council’s utilitarian view of her abilities, Maya’s admiration felt... uncomplicated. Pure.

It was also going to be a problem if Celectra didn’t handle it carefully.

They descended into the cave system, artificial lighting showing the way along frequently traveled passages. Maya stayed close—not quite touching either of Celectra’s bodies, but near enough that her presence registered as warmth in the cool underground air.

“I’ve only been down here once before,” Maya whispered, as if the caves demanded reverence. “Lira said only trained listeners and diplomats were allowed past the first chamber.”

“Usually true,” Celectra confirmed. “But you’re here as my guest, and you have a legitimate purpose. The Galinstanians understand the concept of artists—they shape stone as both communication and expression. They’ll appreciate what you’re trying to do. Speaking of your big sister, I’m thinking of asking her to join the listeners, if she has time. She always had the patience of a diplomat, even when she was babysitting me. We need that here.”

The main communion chamber opened before them, its walls smoothed to flowing curves that caught and amplified the artificial light. Stan was already present—he always sensed her approach through the bismuth network—his quicksilver form pooled in a shallow depression that served as his preferred meeting space.

*Celectra. Welcome.* His thought-voice rippled through her mind like warm water. Then, with curiosity rather than suspicion: *You bring another human.*

*“This is Maya Keahi,”* Celectra said. “She’s an artist. A creator of images. She wishes to document your people visually for cultural exchange.”

Maya had frozen at the chamber’s entrance, eyes wide, one hand clutching her portfolio case like a shield. “He’s... he’s in my head. I can feel him.”

*“That’s normal,”* Celectra assured her gently. *“He’s not reading your thoughts—you’re receiving his. It’s different from what I do. You can’t broadcast back, but you can definitely hear him if you relax and let yourself.”*

Stan flowed closer, not threatening but clearly examining. Maya held perfectly still, and Celectra sensed the younger girl’s nervousness—faint, but unmistakably there.

“Hello,” Maya said shyly, “I’m glad to meet you.”

Celectra translated, then helped Maya spread the sketches on the chamber floor. She examined each drawing and focused on relaying each in turn to Stan.

The pause stretched long enough that Maya began to fidget, shooting worried glances at Celectra—both of her—as if seeking reassurance.

Finally, Stan’s response came, layered with complexity:

Surface layer: *Accurate representation of physical form.*

Deeper layer: *But more than physical—she sees the connection pattern. Sees thought as substance.*

Philosophical layer: *Maya understands that form and consciousness are not separate. This is wisdom many humans lack.*

*“He’s impressed,”* Celectra restated, watching Maya’s face transform again. *“He says you don’t just draw what they look like—you draw what they are. The consciousness behind the form.”*

“Really?” Maya’s voice cracked slightly. “I wasn’t sure if I was getting it right. I’ve never actually seen them except on a tour when I was little; I just worked from descriptions and your notes and—”

*Maya may document the collective,* Stan interrupted gently. *We welcome visual records. Perhaps understanding flows more easily through images than words for some humans.*

Maya looked like she might cry from joy. “Thank you. Thank you so much. I promise I’ll do them justice. I’ll work really hard and—”

*“Breathe,”* Celectra said, both voices warm with amusement. *“You’re already doing them justice. Stan doesn’t offer praise lightly.”*

*“I have to admit,”* said Celectra, *“I came here with the intention of relaxing in the hot pool. Do you think you can draw with your lower half in water?”*

Maya exclaimed, “A hot tub? Really? Down here?

*“Yes, a natural geothermal spring. Very relaxing.”*

“Yeah, if the floor beside it is flat and dry.”

*“It is,”* Celectra confirmed. *“C’mon.”*

Celectra led the way to the grotto, and they both got in—Maya careful to keep her hands dry.

For the next hour, Maya sketched while Celectra facilitated what was essentially an artist’s interview with a liquid metal consciousness. Stan patiently held various poses—compact and dense, spread thin across stone. Maya’s pencil flew across her paper, capturing not just the shapes but the *meaning* behind them with an intuitive understanding that surprised even Celectra.

*Maya has a gift.* Stan observed during one of Maya’s brief breaks to sharpen a pencil. *Different from your gift, Celectra, but valuable. She translates across a different medium—thought into image rather than thought into text.*

*I hadn’t thought of it that way,* Celectra admitted, both bodies settling into comfortable floating positions on the dense mineral water while Maya worked. *But you’re right. It’s still translation. Just visual instead of verbal.*

*Perhaps many humans have gifts for facilitating,* Stan continued. *They simply have not found their medium yet. Maya found drawing. Jax found documentation words. Ananya found pattern recognition. Each pillar is different. All necessary.*

The concept resonated across all of Celectra’s cognitive quadrants, settling into place like a puzzle piece she hadn’t known was missing. She’d been thinking of herself as *the* bridge—unique, irreplaceable, bearing the burden alone. But maybe that was ego talking. Maybe she was just one bridge with many possible piers, each of her peers finding their own way to add stability and support for the bridge.

“Cel?” Maya’s voice pulled her back to the physical world. “Are you okay? You went still for a second.”

*“Just thinking.”* Both her bodies smiled. *“Stan said something wise, and I’m processing it.”*

“He seems really wise.” Maya glanced at Stan’s quicksilver form, then back at her sketch. “And patient. I was worried I’d be bothering him, but he doesn’t seem annoyed at all.”

*Maya worries much,* Stan observed from Celectra’s unintentional and unavoidable transmission. *Seeks approval. Fears rejection.*

Celectra felt the observation land with uncomfortable accuracy. She’d been so focused on Maya’s obvious hero worship that she hadn’t looked deeper at the anxiety beneath it. The fear of not being good enough, of being dismissed, of having her contribution treated as childish rather than valuable.

Familiar fears. Ones Celectra knew intimately.

*“Your work is valuable, Maya,”* she said carefully, choosing her words with precision. *“Stan sees it. I see it. You’re not bothering anyone—you’re contributing something real.”*

Maya’s smile was brilliant but fragile. “Thanks. That means a lot coming from you.”

And there it was—the hero-worship again, naked in her voice. Celectra felt all four hemispheres wince simultaneously. *This needs to be addressed*, the Engineer hemisphere noted. *Before it becomes a bigger problem.*

*But carefully*, the Wildcard hemisphere added. *She’s thirteen and vulnerable. Don’t crush her.*

They emerged from the caves two hours later, Celectra retrieving her hats at the entrance. Maya’s portfolio was now bulging with new sketches and reference materials. The afternoon light painted the settlement in warm coppers, with Niamh hanging low on the horizon while her dimmer companion, Ciara, traced her own arc across the rust-colored sky.

“Thank you for inviting me down there,” Maya said as they walked. She was practically bouncing. “That was the most amazing experience of my entire life. Stan is incredible, and the network is impressive, and you’re amazing at… well, everything, and—”

“Maya.” Both Celectra’s voices carried a gentle firmness. “We should talk.”

The younger girl froze mid-step, excitement collapsing into worry. “Did I do something wrong?”

“No.” Celectra nodded toward a flat rock beside the trail. They sat—Celectra’s bodies bracketing Maya in quiet stereo. “But I think we need to be honest with each other.”

Maya’s hands twisted together in her lap. “About what?”

“About how you see me.” Celectra kept her tone gentle but direct. “I’m not blind, Maya. I can tell you have strong opinions of me.”

The girl’s face flushed crimson. “I don’t—I mean—it’s not—”

“It’s okay,” Celectra said quickly. “I’m not upset, and I’m not teasing you. But I need you to understand something.

She took Maya’s hands—one with each body—grounding the moment. “I like you. You’re talented, kind, and brave enough to put yourself out there. Those are good things.”

Maya swallowed. “…But?”

“I don’t want you to put me on a pedestal,” Celectra said gently. “I’m just a person. A very tired person who happens to have an unusual ability. I’m not a heroine or a miracle.”

“But you seem pretty heroic to me,” Maya whispered.

“And that’s exactly the problem.” Celectra squeezed her hands lightly. “When you put someone on a pedestal, you see the idea of them—not the actual person. And when that idea breaks, it hurts both of you.”

“I don’t have an impossible standard,” Maya murmured.

“Don’t you?” Celectra asked softly. “You called today the most amazing experience of your life. And I know you weren’t just talking about me—Stan was brilliant, and the network did exactly what it was supposed to do. But for me… this was a normal day with friends. A good day, but not magic.”

Maya stared at their joined hands for a long moment. When she spoke, her voice was steadier. “I understand. I’m sorry. I didn’t think about it that way.”

“Don’t apologize for having feelings,” Celectra said. “Just try to see me as real. Not perfect. Not someone to base your life around.”

“But you are special.”

“Special isn’t the same as perfect or untouchable,” Celectra said, smiling wryly. “Special just means different. And different is exhausting.”

That earned a watery laugh. “Okay. I can try. I want to.”

“That’s all I ask.”

“Um, I should tell you though… I don’t just think of you as a heroine,” Maya continued hesitantly. “Celectra? I… I really like you. Like, like-like you. You’re amazing and brave and solar and—”

Celectra gazed in flattered shock at Maya. Her deep, dark eyes, a pair of large, irresistible black holes that made reflected light look like accretion disks, were pulling her in. *Whoa, 180 yaw, maximum thrusters.* Celectra forced herself to snap out of it. Her two voices softened in unison. “I really do like you, too, Maya. You’re sweet, charming, and honestly gorgeous. But I’m with Jax right now. We’re figuring things out.”

Disappointment flickered across Maya’s face.

Celectra continued gently, “If things don’t work out—and if you’re still interested—we can talk. When enough time has passed that nobody gets hurt.”

To soften the sting, she squeezed both of Maya hands gently.

Maya flushed scarlet but managed a small, shy smile. “Okay. I get it. Friends for now?”

“Friends,” Celectra confirmed. “And colleagues. Your work today was incredible, Maya. Truly.”

They sat in companionable silence for a while, watching the twin shadows lengthen across the settlement as both suns continued their descent. Finally, Maya spoke again:

“So, I still help with the documentation project? I know I messed this up with the crush thing, but I really do want to contribute. Not because you’re involved—well, not *just* because you’re involved—but because the work matters.”

The Diplomat: *She’s young and hurting. Be kind.*

The Wildcard: *She’s also resilient enough to hear hard truths. That deserves respect.*

“Yes,” Celectra said. Both her bodies stood, offering hands to help Maya up. “Absolutely yes. Your artwork today was genuinely valuable. Stan meant what he said—you have a gift for visual translation. The project needs you.”

“The project needs me,” Maya repeated, testing the words. “Not you needing me. The work itself.”

“Exactly.” Celectra bumped her shoulder against Maya’s upper arm in a gesture of friendship. “Welcome to the Listener Corps. Your first assignment is to create a visual reference guide of common Galinstanian forms and what they mean. Tomorrow at 0800\. Think you can handle that?”

Maya’s smile was smaller than her earlier brilliant grins, but it reached her eyes with something more genuine. “Yeah. I can handle that.”

They rode their hover-bikes back toward the settlement together, the conversation shifting to technical discussions about artistic techniques and documentation standards. The crush hadn’t vanished—Celectra could still feel it hovering at the edges of Maya’s carefully controlled expression—but it had been acknowledged and, more importantly, contextualized.

Not forbidden. Just... managed.

As they reached the residential cluster where Maya’s family lived, the younger girl paused at her door. “Celectra?”

“Yeah?”

“Thank you for being honest. Even though it sucked to hear.” She smiled crookedly. “Lira always says the truth is supposed to set you free, but she never mentions that it’s uncomfortable first.”

“Your sister really is smart.”

“Don’t tell her that. Her ego’s already insufferable.” Maya stepped toward her door, then looked back one more time. “For the record, though? Even without the pedestal, I think you’re pretty amazing. Just... the regular human amount of amazing. Not the impossible kind.”

She disappeared inside before Celectra could respond, leaving both bodies standing in the copper-tinted evening light, feeling oddly proud of how she’d handled a situation that could have gone badly wrong.

The Engineer: *Crisis averted. Hero-worship acknowledged and redirected toward professional collaboration.*

The Wildcard: *That was hard. Being honest always is.*

Celectra rode home as the last light faded, both bodies tired but satisfied. She would continue training the Listener Corps tomorrow. But tonight, she’d done something different: she’d refused to carry weight that didn’t belong to her. She’d set a boundary with kindness and held it with gentleness. She’d seen a young girl’s potential without accepting the pedestal she offered.

It felt like growth. Uncomfortable, awkward, and absolutely necessary growth.

Both her bodies smiled in the gathering dark, and her mental quartet agreed: she was getting better at this. Slowly, painfully, but definitely better.
