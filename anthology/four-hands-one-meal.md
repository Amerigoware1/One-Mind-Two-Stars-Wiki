The courtyard between the two houses had become their favorite space—stone pavers warm from the day's heat, the Pison River murmuring beyond the low wall, and Chaos rising massive and violet in the darkening sky. Amerigo had set up the outdoor cooking station here, a simple grill and prep table, because he'd missed the ritual of real cooking during all those years on Utopis with nothing but the MRE.
Tonight, he was teaching Celectra to make stir-fry—real vegetables from the hydroponics dome, protein from the colony's small-scale farming operation, and sauce made from scratch instead of synthesized.
"Okay," he said, laying out the ingredients on the prep table. "First rule of cooking: mise en place. That's French for—"
"Everything in its place," Celectra finished, both voices overlapping. "Utara taught me that word last month."
"Of course she did." Amerigo smiled, ruffling one head of white hair. "So, show me. Organize your workspace."
Celectra studied the scattered ingredients—vegetables in a basket, recombinant protein in a sealed container, bottles of sauces and oils, two cutting boards, two knives. Her left body reached for the vegetables while her right grabbed the cutting boards, and she began arranging things with that peculiar efficiency that came from having four hands and a single organizing mind.
Vegetables on the left cutting board, protein on the right. Sauces lined up by order of use. Knives positioned within easy reach. Waste bowl in the center where both bodies could access it.
"Good," Amerigo approved. "Now, the vegetables need to be cut uniform size so they cook evenly. You know what 'uniform' means?"
"The same," both voices said in unison, slightly indignant. "I'm seven, Daddy, not three."
"Seven in Ares years," Zoë interjected with a grin, emerging from the house with a pitcher of iced tea. "Still six in Earth-standard. Practically a baby."
Celectra stuck out both tongues at her, but her eyes were sparkling. "I can cut vegetables. Watch."
She picked up both knives—left hand on the left cutting board, right hand on the right—and Amerigo felt his stomach clench instinctively. "Wait, Cel, maybe start with one—"
But she was already moving. Her left hand sliced a bell pepper into strips with careful, deliberate precision—the kind of attention a seven-year-old could muster when they really, really wanted to prove themselves. Her right hand, simultaneously, diced an onion with the same focused care.
Different tasks. Different techniques. Same level of concentration split perfectly between both bodies.
"How are you doing that?" Zoë breathed, setting down the pitcher.
"Doing what?" Celectra asked, not looking up. Slice, slice, slice. Dice, dice, dice. "Daddy said uniform size, so I'm making them uniform."
Amerigo watched, caught between parental anxiety—those are sharp knives, she's so small—and scientific wonder. Her motor control across both bodies was perfect. Not mirrored, not synchronized, but coordinated. Like watching someone play two different instruments at once and somehow making harmony.
"Just... be careful," he managed. "Those knives are sharp."
"I know. That's why I'm being careful." Her tongue poked out of one mouth in concentration while the other hummed a tuneless melody—one body tense, one relaxed, balancing her focus.
Within five minutes, she had both cutting boards filled with neatly prepared vegetables. The bell pepper strips were uniform. The onion was diced evenly. No cut fingers, no disasters.
She set down both knives with a satisfied sigh and looked up at him expectantly. "What's next?"

Amerigo exchanged glances with Zoë, who was trying not to laugh. "You're a natural," he admitted. "Okay, next is the protein. But this is trickier because—"
"Because protein can carry bacteria if it's not handled right, so I have to wash my hands before and after, use a separate knife, and make sure it's cooked all the way through to at least 74 degrees Celsius," Celectra recited. "Utara had a whole unit on food safety."
"I'm going to have a talk with her about curriculum pacing," Amerigo muttered, but he was smiling. "All right, smarty. Show me."
She washed both sets of hands at the courtyard's small sink—Zoë had to help her reach the soap dispenser—and dried them on a towel. Then she picked up the protein with her left hand, knife in her right, and looked up at him uncertainly.
"This I actually don't know how to do."
"That's okay. That's why I'm teaching you." He moved behind her left body, guiding her hands. "Hold the protein steady with your fingers curled under—see? Like a claw. That protects your fingertips. Then slice with a rocking motion, not sawing."
He demonstrated, and she mimicked with careful attention. Her first slice was tentative, the second more confident.
"Good. Keep going, just like that."
Her right body, meanwhile, stood idle—watching, learning, but not participating. It felt odd to see one of her bodies inactive when the other was engaged.
"What's wrong?" Amerigo asked, noticing her frustrated expression.
"My other hand wants to help, but there's nothing for it to do."
"Then give it something," Zoë suggested. She handed Celectra's right body a whisk and a bowl with sauce ingredients. "Mix this while you cut. Two jobs, two bodies."
Celectra's faces lit up. Now both bodies were engaged—left hand cutting protein, right hand whisking sauce, her attention divided but complete. She worked with renewed enthusiasm, and Amerigo realized this was how she experienced everything. Not sequential tasks but parallel ones, not one thing after another but multiple things at once.
"You're going to be insufferable in the kitchen when you're older," he said fondly. "Cooking entire meals simultaneously."
"Is that bad?" one voice asked while the other hummed.
"No, sweetheart. It's amazing."
The protein cut, the sauce mixed, they assembled everything near the grill—a simple propane-powered unit that ran hot and even. Amerigo heated the cooking surface while Celectra watched, both bodies pressed close to see but not close enough to get burned.
"High heat for stir-fry," he explained. "You want to sear the food fast, not steam it slow. That means—"
"Hot pan, cold oil, food won't stick!" Celectra chimed in. "Utara taught me that, too. It's from an Earth cooking show."
Zoë laughed outright. "She's going to correct your technique by the end of this."
"Probably." Amerigo drizzled oil into the pan, waited for it to shimmer, then nodded to Celectra. "You add the protein first. It takes the longest to cook. But careful—when it hits the oil, it's going to—"
She dumped the protein in confidently and immediately jumped back as hot oil spattered, both bodies flinching in perfect synchronization.
"—sizzle," Amerigo finished dryly. "That's why we stand back."
"You could've warned me!" both voices accused, though she was grinning.
"I was trying to. You were too fast." He showed her how to use the long spatula to move the protein around, keeping it from sticking. "See? You want color on it—that's called the Maillard reaction, it's what makes cooked food taste good."
She took the spatula in one hand, concentrating hard. Her other body held the bowl of vegetables, ready.
"When?" she asked.
"When the protein's about halfway done. You'll see it turn opaque."
She watched intently, both sets of eyes fixed on the sizzling pan. The smell was incredible—garlic, ginger, the rich scent of browning protein. The courtyard filled with it, mingling with the evening air and the distant mineral smell of the river.
"Now?" she asked.
"Now."
She dumped the vegetables in, and immediately the sizzle intensified, steam rising. She stirred enthusiastically, sauce sloshing slightly, and Amerigo resisted the urge to take over. She needed to do this herself—both of her selves.
"Keep it moving," Zoë encouraged from her perch on the courtyard wall. "You want everything coated in that sauce."
Celectra stirred with intense focus, her tongue poking out of one mouth again. The vegetables began to soften, the colors brightening as they cooked. The protein was browning nicely. The sauce was thickening.
"It smells so good," one voice said, awed.
"That's because you're making it yourself," Amerigo told her. "Food always tastes better when you cook it."
After a few more minutes of stirring and adjusting the heat, he deemed it done. "Okay, plates?"
Zoë had already set three plates on the table. Celectra approached the task like a pro—one of her holding the heavy pan steady with two hands, the other spooning out the sauce with painstaking precision. A few drips escaped, but she managed most of it neatly.
They sat around the small courtyard table as Chaos rose higher, its violet light mixing with the last golden rays of Ciara sinking below the ridge. The moons were visible too—Bran glowing orange, Fionn a brilliant point of blue-white, Maeve's rust-red bulk hanging steady.

Celectra took a bite—both bodies at once, using chopsticks she'd been practicing with—and her faces transformed with delight.
"I made this!" both voices exclaimed. "This is mine! I cooked it!"
"You did," Amerigo confirmed, his heart full watching her joy. "All by yourself."
"With help," she corrected generously. "You taught me."
"That's what dads are for."
They ate in comfortable silence for a while, the only sounds the clink of chopsticks and the evening songs of Earth insects imported to Ares—crickets and something that sounded almost like a frog but wasn't.
"Mommy?" Celectra said tentatively—still testing the word, still feeling its weight.
"Yes, sweetheart?" Zoë answered, and the ease of it made Celectra's smile grow.
"Will you teach me to bake? Daddy's good at cooking, but you're good at making things sweet."
"How do you know that?" Zoë laughed.
"Because you always bring pastries when you visit. And they're really good. Better than MRE food."
"Everything's better than MRE food," Amerigo interjected. "That's not a high bar."
"Yours are really, really good though," Celectra insisted. "So will you teach me?"
Zoë reached across the table, taking one of Celectra's small hands in hers. "Of course I will. Tomorrow, if you want. We'll make those berry tarts you like."
"With real berries?"
"With real berries."
Both of Celectra's faces lit up like Fionn catching starlight. She squeezed Zoë's hand and then went back to eating with renewed enthusiasm, already planning what she'd learn, how she'd use her four hands to knead and roll and measure all at once.
Amerigo watched them—his daughter, his partner, this family they were building from broken pieces and unexpected bridges—and felt something settle in his chest. Peace. Belonging. Home.
The three of them finished dinner as darkness fully claimed the sky and Chaos dominated overhead, its storms churning in spectacular silence. Somewhere in the caves beneath them, the Galinstanians were probably gathering for their evening resonance circles. Somewhere on Utopis, the station spun its eternal rotation. Somewhere on Earth—forty-five light-years away—humanity continued on, unaware of this small family finding joy under an alien sky.
But here, in this courtyard with its warm stones and river sounds and the smell of good food cooked with love, Celectra was whole.
Two bodies, one person, learning to cook.
Learning to be.
Simply, perfectly, being.
This life they were cooking up together, one meal at a time.
The house was now truly a home, its stone walls warmed by laughter and love.

"Can we do this every week?" she asked, setting down her chopsticks on two empty plates. "Cooking together?"
"Every week," Amerigo promised.
"Maybe every night," Zoë added. "If you're going to be our sous chef."
"What's a sous chef?"
"An assistant chef. Someone who does the prep work and helps with cooking."
Both of Celectra's chests puffed up with pride. "I'm a sous chef!"
"You are indeed," Amerigo confirmed, standing to clear the plates. "A very talented one. Now, sous chef, help me clean up, and then it's bedtime."
"But I'm not tired!"
"You will be by the time we're done." He handed her a towel. "Dry while I wash."
She grumbled but complied, one body washing dishes alongside him while the other dried and stacked with Zoë. The three of them worked in easy rhythm, a dance they were still learning but getting better at with each repetition.
When the dishes were done and the courtyard cleaned, they headed inside. Celectra's bodies were starting to drag, the exhaustion of concentration and new skills finally catching up.
"Bath, teeth, bed," Amerigo listed. "In that order."
"Will you both tuck me in?" she asked, covering a yawn with one hand while the other rubbed her eyes.
"Both of us," Zoë confirmed. "Always both of us."
As they headed inside, Celectra paused at the doorway and looked back at the courtyard—the empty table, the cooling grill, the vast sky with its moons and planet and stars.
"Today was a good day," she announced.
"It was," Amerigo agreed. "One of the best."
She turned back to them, both faces serious for a moment. "I have good days now because you're both here. Before it was just you, Daddy, and that was good. But now with Mommy too..." She struggled for words. "It's more. Better more."
Zoë knelt down, pulling both bodies into a hug. "It's better more for me too," she whispered. "For all of us."
And as they headed inside to navigate the evening routine—the bath time negotiations, the tooth-brushing supervision, the bedtime story debate—Amerigo thought about how much had changed in the past year. How much they'd survived. How much they'd built.
This family, unconventional and strange, and absolutely right.
This home, under alien stars with alien moons and alien friends in the caves below.
This life they were cooking up together, one meal at a time.
The house was now truly a home, its stone walls warmed by laughter and love.
