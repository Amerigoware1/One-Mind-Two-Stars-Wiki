# Drawing the Line

{% capture chapter_content %}

The Nexus was a wide circular building just like the others around the roundabout in New Eden's center—part community hall, part recreation space—the social hub, its stone walls thick enough to hold the heat on cool evenings and the cool on hot ones. The holo-chambers were on the upper floor, three rooms set aside for entertainment, education, and the occasional therapeutic use that Celectra suggested. The corridor smelled of the same generic cleaning solution as every colony building, but up here the light was better, east-facing windows catching the morning before Chaos rose to dominate the sky.

Celectra was already waiting in the corridor when Zoë arrived, barefoot as always. She had long since dropped the polite excuses about cleaning bots and sanitized floors she’d once used to deflect questions. The truth was that shoes were friction and fire—a recipe for "skin shouting" that her hypersensitive nerves couldn't tolerate. Her two bodies leaning against opposite walls, holopad in one set of hands, specimen bag in the other. The celestite from Oisín. She still brought it sometimes, a talisman rather than a prop now.

"You look like you've been here a while," Zoë said.

"Twenty minutes," Celectra said. "I wanted to think before you arrived."

Zoë had learned to take this seriously. "About the story, or about something else?"

<!-- page -->

"Both." Both faces turned toward her with the particular quality of attention that meant she had been turning something over and was ready to set it down. "I've been thinking about what the shade-beast was. What it really was." A pause. "It wasn't just fear. It was the version of fear that says *you're too small, you don't matter, someone bigger than you gets to decide what happens.* That's why it smelled like Kret."

Zoë was quiet for a moment. "And walking toward it anyway?"

"Was deciding that I got to decide." Both mouths curved, small and certain. "I know that now. I didn't quite know it in that session, but I know it now."

Zoë crouched to her level—one body, then the other—and looked at both faces. "That's the work," she said. "That's exactly the work."

Celectra nodded, satisfied, and tucked the holopad under her arm. "So. We need to finish the story. The Stallion's been waiting long enough."

\*\*\*

They dressed in the chamber's anteroom—Zoë in the leather tunic and moccasins she'd worn in every session, Celectra ordering the holographic leafy dresses and adding the iridescent wings to one body, the furry legs and hooves to the other. Utara loaded the program, and the countryside materialized around them: rolling hills under a warm alien sun, the air carrying the mineral-sweet smell of a world that had never quite decided whether it was meadow or moor.

The hoofprints were already there when they arrived, brighter than before. The muted colors were gone. Whatever had drained them had passed.

*Aquella:* "The prints are fresh. He's close."

*Lolita:* "How close?"

Celectra's winged body rose, hovering above the treeline, and her other body playing Kylyn shaded her eyes to look. "There—in the valley. He's there, but he's not alone."

<!-- page -->

Zoë stepped forward as Lolita. "What do you see?"

"Two armies," Aquella said, her voice dropping. "On either side of a valley. And between them—" a pause— "the Great Grand Stallion. He's standing in the open. Trying to keep them apart by himself."

*Kylyn:* "They'll ride right over him. Neither side will stop for one horse, no matter how great."

*Aquella:* "Then we have to stop them first."

She was already moving—one body dropping from the air, the other sprinting through the long grass—both converging on the valley at speed. Zoë had stopped trying to keep up with Celectra in motion; she ran when she could and narrated the rest.

"The valley opens before you," Zoë said. "Two armies—hundreds of soldiers, banners snapping in a wind that smells of dust and old anger. The ground between them is bare. And in the middle of that emptiness, the Great Grand Stallion stands, white as the moon, larger than any horse you've ever seen. His mane moves in a wind that doesn't touch anything else."

*Aquella, both bodies arriving at the valley's edge simultaneously:* "He knows we're here. He's waiting for us."

She crossed the open ground without slowing, and Utara cooperated—the Stallion turned to face her, his eyes the deep amber of old honey, and something in the chamber's air changed, the way air changed when a window opened.

*Great Grand Stallion*—Utara rendered his thoughts as a deep resonant presence rather than a voice—*"You came. I have been holding this moment as long as I can. But my legs are tired."*

*Aquella:* "Then rest them. We'll hold it now."

She placed one body's hand on the Stallion's nose, her other body turning to face the nearer army, wings spreading wide.

*Aquella:* "Don't move."

<!-- page -->

Zoë watched both armies freeze in the simulation, their commanders' faces registering the impossible arrival of a seven-thousand-year-old water nymph standing between them. Then she felt what Celectra was building toward and stepped back, letting the story breathe.

*Aquella:* "Great Grand Stallion—can you make a line?"

*Stallion:* "A line?"

*Aquella:* "Between them. Something they can see and feel and know is real."

The Stallion reared—Utara made this magnificent, the hooves catching light that didn't exist, the ground trembling in a way the chamber floor managed to suggest—and came down hard, once, and where his hooves struck, a crack ran across the valley floor, rim to rim, straight and absolute. Grass drew back from it. Stone showed through.

Both armies stumbled. Some fell. The charge that had been building in their bones dissolved into something more complicated.

*Aquella, wings folded now, both bodies walking the line:* "Dismount the dragons. Now. Or the Stallion makes another line, and it goes through you."

A long moment. Then the sound of boots on scale, soldiers sliding from the backs of creatures that had not chosen to carry them.

*Aquella:* "Good." She walked to the nearer commander, looked up at him with two sets of eyes at slightly different heights, and said: "Why are you fighting?"

*Commander of the East:* "This land is ours. Our people have farmed it for generations."

She walked to the other side of the line.

*Commander of the West:* "This land is ours. Our ancestors are buried in it."

*Aquella, returning to the center:* "So. Neither of you will give it up."

*East:* "Never."

*West:* "Never."

<!-- page -->

She was quiet for a moment. Zoë recognized the quality of Celectra's pauses now—this was not uncertainty, it was choice. She was deciding how to say the true thing.

*Aquella:* "Then here's what you can do. You can unite. One people. The eastern farmers and the western ones, living in the same land, burying your dead in the same soil, teaching your children each other's histories." She let that sit. "Or you can stay on your own sides of this line. The eastern fields are east of it. The western graves are west of it. The line is real. The Stallion made it. It will not move."

*East, after a long silence:* "You'd leave us divided forever?"

*Aquella:* "I'd leave you with a choice. You can cross the line whenever you want. You just have to decide to do it together."

Zoë felt it—the particular quality the chamber took on when the story landed somewhere true.

*West, quietly:* "And the dragons?"

Aquella walked to the nearest dragon—enormous, golden-scaled, its eyes clouded with the stupor that brain-draining left behind. She placed both hands on its head, one body on each side.

*Aquella:* "I can feel what they did to you. It'll pass."

The scales shifted under her palms. The clouded eyes cleared, slowly, the color of a sky after rain. And then the dragon raised its head and looked at the soldiers around it—and it remembered.

The sound from its throat was not fire. It was something between a roar and a question.

Other dragons began to wake. Soldiers backed away. The commanders reached for weapons.

*Aquella, sharp:* "Leave them alone."

*East commander, alarmed:* "They'll kill us—"

<!-- page -->

*Aquella:* "They won't. Not if you don't give them a reason." She walked between the nearest dragon and the soldiers, both bodies, arms slightly out, a seven-thousand-year-old nymph standing between fear and its consequences. "You won't be brain-draining dragons anymore. You won't be fighting each other anymore. You'll be too busy figuring out how to farm on both sides of a line, and the dragons will go back to being what they were before you decided they were useful."

She looked at the dragon. "Go," she said. "You're free."

Utara lifted it—lifted all of them—spiraling upward in the simulation, their shapes dwindling to specks against a sky that had, while they were attending to the valley floor, turned the particular shade of late afternoon that meant the day had changed into something else.

The two armies stood on either side of the line the Stallion had made. Some were looking at the sky. Some were looking at each other.

*Aquella, quietly, to the Stallion:* "Was that right?"

*Stallion:* "I don't know. But it was honest. That's rarer."

The chime sounded.

The countryside dissolved, and the chamber's white walls returned, and Celectra stood in the middle of the floor—the holographic costumes vanishing with the world—holding both pairs of arms slightly out the way she'd held them between the dragon and the soldiers. She looked at her hands for a moment, then let them fall.

Zoë sat on the chamber floor, back against the wall. Celectra joined her after a moment, both bodies settling, the celestite specimen retrieved from the bag and turned over in her hands.

"That was the end," Celectra said. "Of Aquella's story."

"It was," Zoë agreed.

"I didn't plan it that way. I thought there would be a fight."

"You could have had a fight. You chose not to."

<!-- page -->

Celectra considered this. "It would have been easier in some ways. Fighting is simpler. You win or you lose and it's over." She turned the celestite. "But then the dragons would still be war steeds, and the armies would still be enemies, just—fewer of them."

"So you drew a line instead."

"I gave them a line they already needed," Celectra corrected, gently. "The Stallion made it real. I just—said what it was for."

Zoë watched her hands on the pale blue stone. "That's diplomacy," she said. "Real diplomacy. Not forcing a resolution, just—making the shape of one visible."

Celectra's faces tilted, both at the same angle, considering. "Like the First Contact Committee."

"Exactly like it."

She was quiet for a moment. Then: "I didn't know I was doing therapy when I made up Aquella. I just needed her to be someone who'd lived long enough to know that most things could be talked through." A pause. "I think I needed to believe that was true."

"Did it work?"

Both faces turned to her, the late morning light through the east-facing windows catching the celestite and throwing small blue shadows across her knuckles.

"Yes," she said. "I think it did."

\*\*\*

They left the holo-chamber together, walking the Nexus corridor toward the stairs. Celectra carried the specimen bag in one hand and her holopad in the other, both bodies moving in the easy synchronization of someone who had been entirely themselves for an entire morning.

<!-- page -->

At the top of the stairs, she stopped and looked back through the corridor window. Outside, New Eden was going about its business—people crossing the plaza, a cart from the hydroponic dome, the distant shimmer of the Pison catching the light. And beyond it all, the ridge where the security door sat flush with the rock, leading down to something old and patient and completely indifferent to human timelines.

"Zoë," she said.

"Yes?"

"I'm not going to need Aquella much longer." She said it without sadness, just with the tone of someone making an accurate observation. "I think she's done what she needed to do."

Zoë stood beside her at the window. "What comes next, then?"

Celectra shifted the celestite to the hand that held the holopad, freeing the other to press flat against the glass. She was quiet for a moment, the particular quality of quiet that meant she was reaching—not for anything specific, just orienting, the way you oriented to a familiar signal.

"Something real," she said. "I think I'm ready for something real."
