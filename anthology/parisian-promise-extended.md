The cylindrical elevator car was cramped with six bodies, but Celectra felt only warmth—the solid presence of Uncle Doc at her back, Daddy's hand resting lightly on her shoulder, Dr. Zoë's quiet hum vibrating through the confined space. This closeness wasn't confinement; it was sanctuary.  

"You were incredible in there," Dr. Zoë said softly, her hand finding Celectra's shoulder as the first hint of centrifugal force began to tug at them. "I've never seen such courage."  

"I was terrified," Celectra admitted, her bodies leaning into the growing sensation of weight. The elevator's gentle hum surrounded them as they traveled through the station's skeletal framework.  

“Courage isn’t the absence of fear,” Daddy said, his voice thick with emotion. “It’s doing what’s right despite being afraid.” His eyes met each of the other adults, lingering on Dr. Zoë’s over Celectra’s heads, and something passed between them—pride, wonder, and something deeper.  

“Beautiful speech,” Uncle Doc said lightly, breaking the moment. “If you two start quoting poetry next, I’ll need a bigger elevator.” His grin softened the tension, and even Dr. Zoë laughed, the sound echoing faintly in the narrow space.  

The gravity strengthened steadily as they descended, from the weightlessness of the core to a gentle pull, then stronger still. Celectra felt her feet settling more firmly on the elevator floor, her bodies naturally adjusting to the familiar sensation of weight returning.  

“I think Jaxon really understands now,” she said, catching a glimpse of the habitat ring’s curved hull through the small viewport. “Did you see his face when I untangled his harness?”  

“I saw,” Aunt Dee smiled. “Sometimes showing kindness to someone who’s hurt you is the most powerful thing you can do.”  

“Now that’s a line worth quoting,” Uncle Doc added, earning a playful elbow from Dee.  

The elevator shuddered gently as it locked into the habitat ring’s docking mechanism. The reinforced doors slid open to reveal the curved corridor of the common area, warm light spilling from the observation windows where the party’s finale waited.  

“Ready for cake?” Daddy asked, but when his hand brushed Dr. Zoë’s as they prepared to exit, he drew it back too quickly, as if burned. Celectra caught the exchange, her hearts breaking with matchmaking disappointment.  

“Ready for everything,” Celectra replied, Hope guttered for a heartbeat—then flared brighter.  

\*\*\*  

As the adults gathered near the observation windows, Alex caught Amerigo’s arm. “A word?” he said, his tone friendly but pointed.  

Amerigo glanced toward Zoë, who was already moving toward the refreshment table with Dee. “Sure.”  

Alex steered him to a quieter corner, arms crossed but expression warm. “You’re an idiot.”  

Amerigo blinked. “Excuse me?”  

“Zoë. You’ve been dancing around each other all evening like teenagers at a first dance.” Alex’s grin was knowing. “I’ve been watching you two. The shy smiles, the lingering looks, the way you keep finding excuses to stand next to each other. It’s adorable and painful in equal measure.”  

“We’re just—”  

“Don’t.” Alex held up a hand. “I’ve known you since college, Amerigo. I know what you look like when you’re interested, and I know what you look like when you’re terrified of being interested. Right now, you’re both.”  

Amerigo glanced over at Zoë again. She was laughing at something Dee said, her head thrown back, auburn hair catching the light from the observation windows. Something in his chest tightened.  

“It’s complicated,” he said finally.  

“It’s always complicated. Jenni was my friend too, remember? I miss her.” Alex’s voice softened. “But she wouldn’t want this—you alone, punishing yourself for being alive.”  

“I’m not—”  

“You are. And I know why.” Alex leaned closer. “Because if you don’t let anyone in, you can’t lose them. But Amerigo—you’re not just protecting yourself. You’re protecting Celectra from having someone else who loves her.”  

The words hit harder than Amerigo expected. “What if it doesn’t work out? What if Celectra gets attached and then Zoë realizes this is too much—the medical complications, the stares, the constant worry…”  

“What if it does work out?” Alex countered. “What if Zoë is exactly what you both need, and you miss it because you’re too scared to try?”  

Amerigo was quiet for a long moment, watching Zoë across the room. She caught his eye and smiled—not the professional psychologist smile, but something warmer, more personal.  

“She’s already attached,” Alex said quietly. “Celectra, I mean. Haven’t you noticed? The way she lights up when Zoë’s around and how she asks about her between sessions. That girl is already halfway to calling her ‘Mom,’ whether you two figure your relationship out or not.”  

“That’s what I’m afraid of.”  

“Then stop being afraid and start being honest. With her, with Zoë, with yourself.” Alex clapped him on the shoulder. “Life’s too short to waste it on fear, especially out here. We’re forty-five light-years from Earth on a moon orbiting a gas giant. If we can be brave enough for that, you can be brave enough for this.”  

Amerigo exhaled slowly, then nodded. “When did you get so wise?”  

“Dee’s influence. She’s very insightful.” Alex grinned. “Now go. Stand by that window with her and stop overthinking everything.”  

Amerigo made his way across the common area toward the observation windows. Zoë stood alone now, gazing out at Niamh and Ciara’s eternal dance, their binary orbit casting warm light across her features.  

“Beautiful, isn’t it?” she said as he approached.  

“It is,” he agreed, though he wasn’t looking at the stars.  

\*\*\*  

The transition from zero-G to the habitat ring’s 0.9 \-*g* always took a moment of adjustment. After an hour of floating and spinning in the gym, the centrifugal gravity felt wonderfully grounding to Celectra. After the long, silent ride, the first notes of music felt like sunlight after an eclipse.  

Utara was singing “Parisian Promise”—the lullaby Daddy used to sing to her, a lilting waltz drifting through the lounge. A wobbly guitar, a brushed snare, the soft hum of a synthesized violin. Amerigo had written it for Jenni, Celectra’s mother, whose Persian-Irish heart dreamed of Parisian terraces. It wasn’t the kind of song one expected on a space station, but to Celectra, it was home. “*Sitting out on the terrace, deep in the heart of Paris…*” she hummed through both pairs of lips, her two smiles wide—it was Mommy’s song, and Daddy’s words, even off-key, made the stars feel closer.  

It was the perfect kind of music for Daddy and Zoë.  

The common area had been transformed for the party’s finale. Modular tables arranged in a cheerful cluster, LED strips mimicking starlight patterns along the curved walls, and the great observation windows offering their eternal view of Niamh and Ciara’s dance—two golden stars locked in their gravitational embrace, casting warm light across the gathering.  

At the center of it all sat the birthday cake, a masterpiece of MRE synthesis technology. Purple and blue swirls mimicked a galaxy in miniature, while edible silver dust sparkled like distant stars. Beside it, bowls of “comet” ice cream waited—vanilla studded with Pop-Rocks that crackled and sparked on the tongue like stellar debris.  

“This is the coolest cake ever,” Ananya breathed, her eyes wide with wonder.  

Jaxon stepped forward with an elegant cake server, ready to serve the synthesized confection. His birthday crown had been retrieved from the gym and sat slightly askew on his head, but his smile was genuine now—warm in a way Celectra had never seen before.  

“You want the first piece?” he asked, darting between her faces. Not at her as twins, but at her as... her.  

“Really?” Both her bodies spoke in unison.  

“Really,” Jaxon said, carefully placing a generous slice on a plate. The purple and blue swirls revealed hidden layers—gold veining that caught the binary starlight streaming through the windows. He handed the plate to her left body, then served a second, identical piece for her right.  

“Two bodies, two pieces,” he said with a shy grin. “Same person, but... you probably get twice as hungry, right?”  

The gesture was so thoughtful, so accepting, that Celectra felt warmth bloom in both her chests. “Thank you,” she said softly.  

One of her bodies stayed at the table with the other kids, laughing with Shawanna while Gill and Dev argued over which constellations their glow-in-the-dark stickers belonged to. Zara launched a toy shuttle in looping arcs, shrieking with delight each time it sailed back into her hands.  

Her other body drifted toward the auto-tinted observation windows where the adults gathered, carrying most of her awareness with it. Daddy and Dr. Zoë stood close together, their shoulders nearly touching as they gazed out at the binary stars.  

As *Parisian Promise* hit its bridge—“This feeling deeper than the Seine runs deep, a sacred promise that our hearts will keep”—Celectra’s left body swayed slightly, the lullaby’s rhythm echoing Jenni’s love for Paris. She glanced past Daddy. Near the refreshment table, Uncle Doc had his hand resting on Aunt Dee’s lower back. They were swaying gently to the music, barely moving their feet—a slow, contented dance that required no attention. It was a small, private moment of ease amidst the party chatter.  

“It’s beautiful here,” Zoë murmured. “I can see why Celectra loves those stars so much.”  

“She calls them her inspiration,” Daddy replied, his voice carrying that special tenderness he reserved for her. “Says they prove that being different doesn’t mean being alone.”  

Zoë turned toward him, smiling at his words. “She’s wise beyond her years. Both of them—her.” She chuckled softly at her own correction. “I’m still getting used to the right pronouns.”  

“She is remarkable,” Daddy agreed, his tone dropping lower. “I was worried about today. About how the other kids would react.”  

“Children are often more accepting than we give them credit for,” Zoë said, glancing toward the table—toward Celectra’s other body. “Especially when someone has the courage to show them who they really are.”  

Their hands brushed as they both reached for drinks, and this time, neither pulled away. The contact sent a flutter of hope through Celectra’s observant hearts, linking her two vantage points together in a rush of warmth.  

“You know,” Daddy said, his voice carrying a note of wonder, “I haven’t seen her this confident in... well, maybe ever.”  

With both bodies grinning, Celectra shifted more of her awareness to the table, letting the conversation of the adults fade into the background. Her body near the adults turned, walking toward the laughter, as she drew her bodies back together.  

The conversation at the table had turned to party favors. Gill and Dev were comparing glow-in-the-dark star stickers, arguing about which constellations they represented. Zara had claimed a small toy shuttle that actually flew in graceful arcs when you pressed its thrusters.  

“These are for you,” Jaxon said, approaching Celectra with two identical packages. Inside each was a holographic star chart—not unlike the gift she’d brought for him, but showing the view from Utopis at this exact moment.  

“So you can always remember tonight,” he explained, his cheeks pink with embarrassment. “And... and I’m sorry. For being mean before. I thought you were two people being better than me, but knowing you’re one person...” He paused, searching for the right words. “It just makes you more amazing, not less.”  

Celectra clutched the star charts to both her chests, emotions threatening to overwhelm her. “I have something for you too,” she said, retrieving her carefully prepared gift—the holographic map of the Niamh-Ciara system.  

Jaxon activated the device, and miniature binary stars bloomed above his palm, their gravitational dance playing out in perfect detail. “Whoa,” he breathed. “They really do orbit each other.”  

“Around their barycenter,” Celectra explained, both bodies leaning in. “The point where their gravity balances. They’re separate but connected, different but working together.”  

“Like you,” Jaxon said, understanding dawning in his blue eyes.  

“Like me,” she confirmed.  

Around them, the party was winding down. Parents were gathering belongings, and kids were trading final stories and promises to play together again soon. But Celectra felt like she could float even in the habitat ring’s solid gravity—not because of zero-G, but because of the lightness in her hearts.  

She caught Daddy’s eye across the room, where he stood with Dr. Zoë, along with Aunt Dee and Uncle Doc, by the great windows. Both adults were smiling at her, and she realized they’d been watching the exchange with Jaxon. Daddy’s expression held such pride, such love, that she had to resist the urge to run to him for a hug.  

Instead, she whispered to herself, just loud enough for her enhanced hearing to catch, “Dr. Zoë really likes you, Daddy.”  

The binary stars continued their eternal dance outside the windows, two separate lights that created something beautiful together. Watching them, surrounded by friends who finally knew her truth, Celectra felt ready for anything.  

She was Celectra, two bodies sharing one brilliant mind, and she had people who loved her exactly as she was.  

Outside, the binary stars kept circling their shared light. Inside, Celectra breathed in sync with them—whole, loved, and endless as the night.
