Title: The In-Between Part

Celectra woke on her eighth birthday to the smell of omelets and the pale gold light filtering through her window. Both of her padded barefoot across the cool stone floor, still drowsy, still drinking her morning shake, the familiar hum of her celectricity a soft background thread connecting her two bodies. Daddy stood at the counter with his back to her, doing something with his hands.  
“Morning, birthday girl,” he said without turning. “Eight years old. That’s official now.”  
“Finally,” Celectra said, her voices still thick with sleep. She’d been waiting for this age forever—eight felt solid, a real number, not a little-kid number anymore.  
Then he turned, and she saw the small box on the counter.  

<!-- page -->

*What is that?* Curiosity prickled through both of her, the sleepiness evaporating. He opened it, and there they were: two pairs of earrings—lightning bolts, four of them, carved from diamonds that weren’t the usual icy white but a warm, yellowish gold that caught the morning light and threw sparks—at least three carats apiece. They looked like captured electricity.  
“Come here,” he said, his dark chocolate eyes warm.  
She approached, one body on each side of him, her four feet nearly silent. She stared at the earrings, her breath catching. Lightning bolts. Like the celectricity that hummed between her—the invisible thread that made two bodies into one person.  
“Like my celectricity,” she whispered, not meaning to say it aloud but unable to hold it in. The shape, the gold flash—he’d seen it somehow, the thing she felt but couldn’t draw, couldn’t explain.  
“Exactly like your celectricity,” he confirmed. “The diamonds are a little different, though. I asked Stan if I could collect some of what they'd pushed aside. They’re the insoluble leavings of their feeding, the purest carbon crystals you can imagine, with a golden spark from unique lattice distortions fused within the carbon.”  
Galinstanian diamonds. The words sent a little thrill through her. Stan and the others had given these, a product of their strange, beautiful biology. The gems weren’t just rocks—they were a gift from the deep, from minds that had always seen her as one being.  
Daddy’s voice went quieter. “I figured if anything should represent what makes you unique, it’s lightning.”  

<!-- page -->

*Unique. One. Not twins. Not Celeste-and-Electra. Just me.*  
Celectra remembered the old earrings she’d worn since she was tiny—gold stars for one body, amber teardrops for the other. Mismatched, because that was what people expected when they saw two faces. A few months ago she’d swapped them, wearing one star and one teardrop on each body, a silent message she wasn’t sure anyone would read. Daddy had noticed. He’d actually noticed.  
Her throat tightened. “Daddy…” both her voices came out wobbly.  
“You get to decide what to do with the old ones,” he went on. “Keep them, pass them on, whatever you want. These are for the you you’ve always been—one person with her own kind of electricity running between her.”  
She threw all four of her arms around him, pressing both her faces against his chest. His shirt smelled like coffee and the faint, clean scent of the lab. She could feel his heartbeat under her right cheek, steady and solid. “Thank you, Daddy!” she managed, her voices muffled. “Thank you, thank you!”  
He held her close, his hands warm on both her backs. “Happy birthday, Celectra. Your Mommy, Jenni, would be so proud of who you’ve become.”  
Mommy. The name stirred a hollow ache, but a gentle one—like pressing a bruise that’s nearly healed. Celectra couldn’t remember her face from her own memory, only from pictures, but she carried Jenni in her cells, in the fact that she was alive at all. Daddy’s words wrapped around that ache and made it feel less like loss and more like a gift.  

<!-- page -->

When she pulled back, wiping her eyes with both sets of hands, Zoë appeared in the doorway. She had a knowing smile, the kind that meant she’d been listening.  
“Did he give you the earrings?” Zoë asked.  
“You knew?”  
“He’s been obsessing over getting them perfect for three whole weeks,” Zoë said, moving into the kitchen. “He discarded a fortune in diamonds—Galinstanian diamonds!—just to get the precise angle on the bolt. It was all about maximizing the electric tone in the stone to capture the ‘celectricity’ effect when you move.”  
Celectra looked at the earrings again, the lightning cradled in the box. Three weeks. He’d been planning this, cutting and discarding priceless gems, all to make something that would show the world what she felt inside. Her chest went tight and full at the same time. “Because you see me,” she said, and the wonder in her own voices surprised her. “As one person. Not Celeste and Electra. Just Celectra and her celectricity.”  
She watched the words land in him. His throat moved. “That’s exactly right, sweetie.”  
She wanted to put them in immediately, wanted to see them in her ears, matching, whole. “Can you help me?” she asked Zoë.  
“Of course.”  
They sat at the kitchen table while Daddy started the omelets. Zoë’s fingers were careful as she removed the old earrings—the gold stars and amber teardrops that had marked Celectra as “twins” for eight years. A strange lightness came when they were gone, like taking off a costume she hadn’t realized she’d been wearing. Zoë replaced them with the new lightning bolts, first in her left body’s ears, then her right. The posts were cool, then warmed quickly against her skin.  

<!-- page -->

Celectra held the old earrings in one palm—her left body’s hand, she thought, though the distinction barely mattered. The star and the teardrop. They’d been with her so long. “I think I’ll keep these,” she said softly. “They’re part of my story. Just not the whole story anymore.”  
*Like the scar from a surgery,* she thought, *or memories of a clinic. You don’t erase the hard parts just because you’ve healed.*  
“That’s wise,” Zoë said, and Celectra felt her pride like a warmth against her skin. She sat back. “Perfect. What do you think?”  
Celectra turned both her heads, each face looking at the other. Four ears, each with a lightning bolt, the golden diamonds flickering in the light. Matched. The same symbol on both bodies. She looked like… herself. Not two versions of one person. Just one person, symmetrically expressed. The lightning bolts seemed to dance when she moved, and the hum of her celectricity pulsed a little brighter, as if it recognized itself.  
“I think,” she said slowly, feeling the words settle into truth, “that this is the best birthday present anyone’s ever given me.”  
Daddy pulled her close again, and she melted into the hug. “You deserve to be seen for who you are.”  
She felt those words in her bones. For so long, even well-meaning people had said things like "the girls" or "both of you"—as if she were plural. But this present said, "One person, one electricity, one daughter." It was a gift beyond the diamonds.  

<!-- page -->

Zoë disappeared for a moment and came back with another package, smaller and simpler. “Now, this isn’t as flashy as Daddy’s,” she said, handing it over. “But I think it’ll keep you busy.”  
Celectra unwrapped it eagerly—both sets of hands working the paper, her coordination seamless without thought—and found an art kit. Two sketchpads, colored pencils, a tiny tin sharpener. Real paper. Real pencils. Her dual hearts gave a little leap. She loved drawing on the holopad, but real paper had a smell, a texture, a permanence.  
“Real everything,” Zoë said, brushing a lock of white hair behind one ear. “Tactile creativity builds focus and patience. Besides, not everything in life should have an undo button.”  
Celectra clutched the kit to her chest, both her faces splitting into grins. No undo button. She liked that. Some things should last, mistakes and all. “Thank you so much!”  
She meant it with the whole of her.  
Daddy watched them, and Celectra saw his hand reach for Zoë’s, their fingers intertwining over her back. His eyes were wet. Her heart felt so full it might burst—full of him, and her, and the mommy she’d never met but carried with her always. *This is what family feels like,* she thought. *Not a single unchanging shape, but an orbit that expands to hold everyone you love.*
She wanted to show them—needed to show them—the map she'd been drawing in secret, the one that made sense of all of it.  

<!-- page -->

She darted to her room, her four feet pattering, and returned with her holopad. The 3D diagram glowed up at them: a galaxy with “Celectra” as a binary pair, “Daddy” and “Mommy Zoë” as main sequence stars, “Mommy Jenni” as a neutron star—small and dense and full of energy—all orbiting a shared barycenter—the invisible point between them that Celectra had labeled, simply, 'Us.' She’d drawn it in secret weeks ago, hoping.  
Daddy laughed, his voice thick. “You’ve been planning this, haven’t you?”  
“Forever,” Celectra said, her dual voices smug. And she had.  
While Zoë began brushing her hair—an amazing sensation, the brush working through the tangles with gentle precision—Celectra opened the art kit. Two sketchpads. She set one before her left body and one before her right.  
Her left body picked up a pencil and started drawing the Pison River from memory: the way the water looked on her birthday swim last year, crystal-clear, the rocks smooth and gray, and sun-warm. She sketched the curve of the bank, the fruit trees leaning over with their gnarled branches, the diamond ripples where the light hit the surface. Each stroke felt deliberate and real, capturing a place she loved exactly as it was.  
Her right body, meanwhile, moved into something stranger. Circles within circles, connected by flowing lines. Orbits. Electrons around nuclei. Stars around barycenters. Her two bodies linked by invisible threads of electricity. She didn’t plan it; her pencil seemed to know where to go before her brains did. The shapes emerged like a language she was learning to speak.  
*What does it mean?* she wondered. It wasn’t a place like the river. It was a feeling, a truth about how she was put together. The overlapping circles at the center formed a Venn diagram shape, and in the middle part—the largest part—she drew a small figure. Herself. The in-between space where her two selves met.

Daddy passed by the table and paused. “Those are beautiful, Cel. Very different, though.”  
“Different how?” she asked without looking up. Her left hand shaded the river rocks; her right added detail to the orbital lines. The two tasks felt effortless, her attention flowing between them like the current of the river she was drawing.  
“Well, one’s realistic, one’s abstract. One’s a place, one’s…” He tilted his head, studying. “…a concept? A feeling?”  
Celectra stopped and looked at both drawings with both sets of eyes. He was right. One was the world outside; the other was the world inside. But they didn’t feel separate. They were both true.  
“They’re both me,” she said simply.  
He stood there a moment longer, and Celectra felt his understanding click into place—not complete, maybe, but closer than it had ever been. “Yes,” he said quietly. “I suppose they are.”  
Zoë squeezed both her shoulders gently, one hand on each. “What’s that one called?” she asked, pointing to the abstract.  
“My celectricity,” Celectra said. “The in-between part—that’s me.”  
“I like it,” Zoë said. “A lot.”  

<!-- page -->

A knock came just as Daddy was plating breakfast. Zoë opened the door to Uncle Doc and Aunt Dee, both carrying wrapped gifts. Aunt Dee’s and Uncle Doc’s smiles were broad.  
“Morning,” Uncle Doc said brightly. “We brought presents for the birthday girl.”  
Aunt Dee handed Celectra a package, and she took it with her right body, careful and curious. Inside was a book: *The Secret Lives of Plants*. The cover was beautiful: leaves and roots and hidden blossoms. But when she opened it, something slipped out—two pressed flowers, delicate and perfect, one for each of her hands to hold.  
“From my garden dome,” Aunt Dee explained, her voice still carrying that musical roughness. “I thought… I thought you might like to start your own collection. Of beautiful things that grow despite difficult conditions.”  
Celectra looked down at the flowers. Even dried, their colors glowed—purple and gold, tiny veins still visible in the petals. They’d been alive, and now they were preserved, still beautiful, still themselves. *Like me,* she thought. *A thing that grew when the conditions weren’t easy. A thing that survived.*  
“Like me,” she said softly.  
“Exactly like you,” Aunt Dee confirmed. “Beautiful, strong, and absolutely one-of-a-kind.”  
Warmth spread through Celectra from both her centers. She smiled with both her faces, genuine and full. “Thank you, Aunt Dee. I love it. And I love you both.”  
“I love you too, sweetie,” Aunt Dee said, kissing the top of her left head.  
Uncle Doc repeated, “I love you too, sweetheart,” and kissed her right.  

<!-- page -->

Then Daddy pointed her toward the corner, where two large, wrapped packages waited. Celectra’s four hands made quick work of the paper—and there they were. Two gel blobs, identical to the ones in the Medical Center room, but here, in her home, wrapped in turquoise microfiber, looking soft and welcoming instead of sterile and clinical.  
Nurse Fairchild had remembered.  
“Nurse Fairchild remembered!” she exclaimed with one voice while her other body was already plopping onto a blob, sinking into its adaptive comfort. The gel molded around her body, cool and supportive, exactly like she remembered from the good moments in that office—the moments before the bad ones.  
*The clinic*, she thought, and a shadow flickered through her mind—the tables, the pulling, the gray emptiness. But the blob held her steady. Here, in her house, it wasn’t medical equipment. It was a throne. A safe place. *Mine.*  
Her other body joined the first, and both of her settled into the blobs with a sigh of pure delight. “They’re perfect. They’re going in my room!”  
From the card, Daddy read: “You said you wanted some for home. And after everything… well, I wanted you to have something from that office that could be a good memory instead.”  
Reclaiming comfort. That’s what this was. Taking a thing associated with fear and separation and turning it into something joyful. Celectra wiggled deeper into the blobs and felt the past loosen its grip a little more.  
Zoë squeezed Daddy’s hand, and Celectra saw it with her left body’s peripheral vision. They understood what this meant—maybe not the full weight of it, but enough.

After breakfast, they made the trek down to the caves.  
The moment Celectra stepped inside, her thoughts touched the bismuth strand that ran the length of the path. It was like dipping into a river—the Galinstanians’ web humming with slow, patient awareness. *Hello,* she thought toward them and felt a pulse of welcome back. One of her bodies darted ahead, eager, while the other held back, gripping Daddy’s hand with her right and Zoë’s with her left. She didn’t feel stretched thin anymore. She felt elastic, flexible. Both here, both connected.  
Down in the sculpted chambers, Stan and several others emerged from the crevices. Their forms shimmered in the lamplight like living metal, beautiful and strange.  
*It’s Celebration Day,* Stan transmitted, his thoughts warm with pleasure. *We sense happiness in you, Celectra.*  
She touched her new earrings, both her left and right hands moving in perfect mirror. The lightning bolts were cool under her fingers. *“Daddy gave me these new earrings. Lightning bolts—for my celectricity. So I match. So people see me as me.”*  
*We have always seen you as you,* came the gentle reply. *But we understand symbols matter to your kind. We were happy to provide the indigestible stones. They are beautiful—we sense the intense, static charge of their structure, and we feel the beauty you see in them. They are like captured sparks. Come, I have something to show you.*  
Her dual curiosity sharpened. “Wait! How did… how did you ask for the diamonds, Daddy?”  
As they walked behind Stan, Daddy explained. “I didn’t. Remember when Alex and I went to see the Galinstanian’s discarded waste? You didn’t want to see it." He grinned, “One species' trash is another species’ treasure.”  
They gave them willingly. That knowledge sent a fresh wave of warmth through Celectra. Her earrings weren’t just stones. They were a gift from two species, connected through her.  

<!-- page -->

Stan led them into a new chamber, and the moment she crossed the threshold, something changed. The air felt different—thick, alive, buzzing against her skin like a million tiny sparkles. The walls were striated with veins of polished bismuth, gleaming with their cool crystalline sheen, arranged in an intricate, repeating geometric pattern.  
And then her celectricity sang.  
The background hum that always connected her two bodies, normally a soft, distant thing she could ignore, suddenly swelled into a powerful, clear, singing force. It wasn’t louder, exactly—it was cleaner. Sharper. Every thread of connection between her bodies vibrated in perfect harmony, like a note hit dead center.  
She lifted a hand toward the wall, tracing the cool intersection of the veins. The geometry… *“The bismuth vein geometry…”* she breathed out.  
Stan’s thoughts rippled with anticipation. *We calculated the dimensions from observing your field patterns. Correct?*  
Celectra felt both her faces shift into expressions of stunned comprehension. Her mind raced, piecing it together. The node spacing. The resonant frequency. *“You’ve arranged it as a standing wave resonator,”* she realized aloud. *“The node spacing matches my interbody signal frequency exactly—approximately 21.7 Hz. You built me a neural amplifier.”* She turned, trying to take in the whole chamber. *“Perfect,”* she whispered. *“This is applied quantum biology. You’re using constructive interference to boost my celectricity. That’s brilliant.”*  
Brilliant. It was more than brilliant. It was a gift beyond anything she’d imagined. They’d studied her—not as a specimen, but as a friend—and built this place specifically for her frequency, her connection. A wave of profound joy washed over her, so powerful it made her eyes sting.  
*Thank you,* she broadcast, the thought crisp and resonant, amplified by the very chamber around her. *For seeing me. For helping me. For being my friends.*  
The response came as a chorus—dozens of minds joining in harmony: *We are honored to know you, bridge-between-worlds.*  
Bridge-between-worlds. The title settled over Celectra like a mantle. Heavy, but right.

Evening came, and the courtyard between the two houses glowed with warm stone and the soft, coppery light of the binary stars. They’d dipped below the horizon, but their afterglow still illuminated the giant planet and its moons—giant Chaos, violet and swirling, casting a faint warm light from overhead. The mix of copper and violet made the world look enchanted.  
Tonight, Daddy was teaching Celectra to make stir-fry. Her first real dinner.  
“Everything in its place,” she recited as she arranged the ingredients with uncanny precision: vegetables to the left, protein to the right, sauces lined up by order of use. Her four hands moved in a choreography she didn’t have to think about—one body slicing, the other organizing, the rhythm flowing back and forth.  
“Utara taught me that,” she added proudly. She loved that she could finally use her lessons—she’d watched and practiced and now, with real food and real fire, everything clicked.  
Daddy guided her through each step, his voice patient, his hands steadying the pan when it wobbled. The sizzle of oil, the fragrant steam, the way the vegetables softened and brightened—Celectra soaked it all in through two sets of senses, the experience richer for being doubled. Zoë leaned against the wall with a pitcher of iced tea, teasing them gently, while Uncle Doc and Aunt Dee drifted over from next door, drawn by the laughter.  
When it was time to serve, Celectra held the pan steady with her right body while her left spooned the stir-fry onto plates. A few drips escaped, but no one minded.  
She took her first bite with both mouths simultaneously—the flavors blooming, savory and fresh. “I made this!” she said, her voices bright with wonder. “I really made this!”  
“You did,” Daddy said, smiling.  
“With help,” she corrected. “We made it.”  
Dinner stretched into easy conversation, the kind that wraps around a person like a blanket. Zoë promised to teach her baking next—real baking, with yeast and rising dough—and Aunt Dee offered berry preserves from her garden dome. Uncle Doc declared her knife skills “better than half the med staff,” which made Celectra sit up straighter.  
Under Chaos’s violet glow, they shared food and stories. Celectra let herself sink into the moment, feeling the weight of her eight years as something solid and good. She wasn’t two halves trying to keep up. She was one girl, whole and loved, cooking for her family on their shared world.  

<!-- page -->

The meal wound down. Mommy brought out a cake she’d baked completely by hand, her bismuth ring glinting as she passed slices around. As she leaned back to laugh at something Daddy said, Celectra’s eyes caught the gentle bulge at her middle—small, but unmistakable.  
A baby. Her sibling. A new star forming in their family galaxy.  
Celectra smiled, a private, inward thing on both her faces. Their orbits were expanding again.  
Daddy’s voice broke into her thoughts. “Penny for your thoughts?”  
Celectra smiled, both faces turning to meet his dark chocolate eyes—so like her own. “Just thinking about how far we’ve come. How different everything is from a year ago.”  
“Better different?” Mommy asked.  
“Much better, Mommy,” Celectra confirmed. “Scary sometimes, but good scary. Growing scary.”  
“That’s the best kind,” Uncle Doc said, raising his glass. “Here’s to growing scary. To new beginnings. To bridges between worlds.”  
They all raised their glasses—even Celectra with her juice—and clinked them together. The sound rang bright in the warm air.

Later, when the copper dusk had faded and Chaos rose violet in the darkening sky, Celectra stood at her bedroom window. Her gel blobs sat in the corner, already looking like they belonged. Her new sketchpads lay on her desk, the drawings of the river and her celectricity side by side.  
She touched her lightning bolt earrings. The golden diamonds caught the last light, throwing tiny sparks into the dim room.  
*This is my life now,* she thought. In the other room, her family—Daddy, Mommy, and the tiny new star growing between them. Next door, Uncle Doc and Aunt Dee. Deep below, in chambers carved from living rock, the Galinstanians continuing their slow, patient existence, knowing they weren’t alone.  

<!-- page -->

The nightmares still came sometimes—the memory of those tables pulling apart, the gray emptiness where half of her should have been. But they came less often now. When they did, she had Daddy and Mommy to hold her through, their arms around both her bodies, their voices anchoring her to the present.  
She had survived. More than that—she had thrived.  
Outside, a meteor streaked across the darkening sky. Celectra watched it with both sets of eyes, tracing its silver arc.  
Different pieces, different orbits, but one system, she thought. One home.  
For once, she let herself simply be. Not an ambassador. Not a marvel. Just Celectra. One person, two bodies, four hands holding her family close.  
Whole. Home. Loved.
