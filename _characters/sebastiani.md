## Overview

Lidia Sebastiani is the council member responsible for energy systems and grid infrastructure. A brilliant electrical engineer with a background in fusion reactor design, she approaches every problem through the lens of risk assessment and system redundancy. During the Galinstanian disclosure, she emerged as the primary skeptic, voicing concerns that others were too relieved or too young to articulate.

## Physical Description

Sebastiani carries herself with the precise posture of someone who has spent decades in clean rooms and control centers. Her dark hair shows distinguished silver at the temples, and she wears it pulled back in a severe bun that emphasizes her sharp cheekbones. Her eyes are pale gray, almost colorless, and they have a habit of narrowing slightly when she's processing information—a tell that her colleagues have learned to recognize. She favors functional clothing in dark colors, typically a charcoal jumpsuit with the colony's engineering insignia.

## Personality & Skills

**Analytical Rigor:** Sebastiani doesn't trust anything she can't measure, model, or stress-test. This makes her invaluable for identifying failure modes but occasionally frustrating for those trying to build consensus around uncertain situations.

**Risk Assessment:** Her greatest strength is also her defining limitation: she sees threats before opportunities. During the Galinstanian disclosure, while others focused on the miracle of contact, she immediately calculated worst-case scenarios for infrastructure vulnerability.

**Technical Expertise:** She designed the colony's power distribution network and maintains intimate knowledge of every transformer, every conduit, every backup system. If the lights stay on during a crisis, Sebastiani is why.

**Uncompromising Honesty:** She will not soften bad news or dress up uncomfortable truths. This makes her difficult to like but impossible to dismiss.

## Relationships

- **Amerigo Carisco:** Their ideological clash during the Galinstanian disclosure became legendary. Sebastiani warned of betrayal; Amerigo argued for trust. History would eventually vindicate one of them.
- **Santos:** Respects her leadership but frequently pushes back on what she perceives as Santos's willingness to accept uncertainty.
- **Celectra:** Views the child with protective skepticism—not distrust of Celectra herself, but of the situation that placed such responsibility on someone so young.
- **Yamoto:** Finds common ground in procedural rigor, though Yamoto's approach is more diplomatic.

## Story Overview

### Book 1 Arc

Sebastiani appears primarily during council sessions, where she serves as the voice of caution. Her role peaks during the Galinstanian disclosure in Chapter 27, where she articulates the fears that others suppress:

> "We're talking about beings that can dissolve rock. That can travel through our foundations invisibly. That have been listening to every transmission we've sent for seventeen years. I want someone to explain to me why that isn't an existential threat."

When Celectra counters her arguments, Sebastiani subsides—not convinced, but outargued. She abstains from the final vote rather than opposing it outright, a compromise that speaks volumes about her character.

Her final scene in Book 1 is a warning delivered to Amerigo as the chamber empties:

> "When those things turn on us," she said, "and they will—I hope you'll remember I said so."

> "When you're proven wrong," Amerigo replied, "I hope you'll have the grace to say that too."

This exchange establishes the tension that will define her arc: she is not a villain, but a guardian whose vigilance may prove either prescient or paranoid.

### Book 2 Setup

In Book 2, Sebastiani's role expands as the colony navigates the practical challenges of coexistence. Her expertise becomes crucial when:

- Mapping safe mining zones that don't infringe on Galinstanian territory
- Designing power systems that account for subterranean neighbors
- Developing early-warning protocols for infrastructure anomalies

Her arc explores whether her skepticism was wisdom or prejudice—and what happens when a person built for crisis must learn to live in peace.

## Key Moments

1. **The Disclosure Challenge:** She forces the council to confront uncomfortable questions about vulnerability, ensuring that coexistence protocols include genuine safeguards rather than wishful thinking.

2. **The Abstention:** By abstaining rather than voting no, she acknowledges the strength of the opposing argument while maintaining her own position—a nuanced stance that reflects her intellectual honesty.

3. **The Warning:** Her parting words to Amerigo establish her as a Cassandra figure, someone whose warnings may haunt the narrative regardless of their accuracy.

## Philosophy

*"Trust is a luxury. Redundancy is survival."*

Sebastiani believes that hope without preparation is negligence. Her worldview was shaped by Earth's history of catastrophic infrastructure failures—collapses that killed thousands because someone trusted the wrong variable. To her, caution isn't cynicism; it's responsibility.
