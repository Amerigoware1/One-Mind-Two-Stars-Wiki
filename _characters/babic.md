## Overview

Rhea Babić is the council member responsible for orbital mechanics, navigation, and system-wide coordination. A former cartographer and aerospace engineer from Croatia, she specializes in translating complex spatial data into actionable maps and protocols. Her work became essential during the Galinstanian surveys, where her ability to visualize three-dimensional subsurface territories helped establish boundaries that respected both human needs and Galinstanian sovereignty.

Her arc explores the challenge of drawing boundaries between worlds that occupy the same physical space—how do you map coexistence when two species share a moon but experience it differently?

</div>

<div class="card-bg" markdown="1">

## Physical Description

Babić has the lean build of someone who spent years in low-gravity simulation training. Her dark brown hair is cut short and practical, and she wears it with a slight wave that suggests she doesn't spend much time worrying about appearance. Her eyes are warm brown, often crinkled at the corners from habitual squinting at displays and star charts. She favors comfortable layers—soft sweaters over flight suits—and always carries a stylus tucked behind her ear. There's a small scar on her left cheekbone from a childhood accident she refuses to explain.

</div>

<div class="card-bg" markdown="1">

## Personality & Skills

**Spatial Intelligence:** Babić thinks in three dimensions. She can mentally rotate complex structures, predict orbital trajectories, and visualize subsurface cavern networks with equal ease. This makes her invaluable for mapping Galinstanian territory.

**Diplomatic Precision:** Unlike Sebastiani's confrontational skepticism, Babić expresses concerns through careful questions that guide others to conclusions. She's a consensus-builder who respects dissent.

**Patience:** Years of working with orbital mechanics—where timing matters more than speed—have given her an almost meditative calm. Crisis doesn't rattle her; it focuses her.

**Technical Versatility:** She's equally comfortable discussing fusion drive maintenance, cave survey methodology, and diplomatic protocol. This breadth makes her a bridge between specialties.

</div>

<div class="card-bg" markdown="1">

## Relationships

- **Sebastiani:** They disagree frequently but respect each other deeply. Babić often translates Sebastiani's bluntness into language the council can hear.
- **Roan:** Collaborated closely on survey protocols; their working relationship is built on mutual competence and shared commitment to accuracy.
- **Celectra:** Treats the child as a full professional partner, never talking down or oversimplifying. Celectra trusts her implicitly.
- **Troy:** Shares his appreciation for methodical science; they often consult each other on technical matters outside their portfolios.

</div>

<div class="card-bg" markdown="1">

## Key Moments

1. **The Mapping Initiative:** She volunteers to lead the dangerous work of subsurface surveying, knowing that accurate maps are the foundation of peaceful coexistence.

2. **The Babić Projection:** Her innovative mapping system becomes the standard for all future territorial negotiations, earning recognition from both humans and Galinstanians.

3. **The Corridor Agreement:** She negotiates the first formal passage rights through Galinstanian territory, establishing precedent for all future interactions.

</div>

<div class="card-bg" markdown="1">

## Philosophy

*"Maps don't create borders. They reveal where borders already exist."*

Babić believes that understanding precedes agreement. You cannot negotiate territory you cannot visualize, and you cannot respect boundaries you cannot measure. Her work is driven by the conviction that clarity—however difficult—is kinder than ambiguity.

</div>

- "Coexistence isn't a line. It's a volume."

- **Visualization as Understanding:** How do we comprehend realities that don't match our sensory experience?
- **Boundaries and Connection:** Can borders enable relationship rather than prevent it?
- **The Ethics of Mapping:** Who has the right to name and define territory?
