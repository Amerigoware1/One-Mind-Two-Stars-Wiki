Auto-Otto (automatic induction charger)

Pronunciation: /ˈɔːtoʊ ˈɒtoʊ/

A magnetic induction charging ottoman. A soft landing pad for heels that is firm enough to charge devices without them slipping off.

Form

Sixty centimetres deep, one hundred and ten long, forty-two tall — standard ottoman height, long enough for two people or one person and a sprawl of devices. The profile is a wedge: a flat top that runs the first twenty-six centimetres, then a ramp falling away at a constant twenty-five degrees for the remaining thirty-four.

The flat top is the charging surface. A sixteen-centimetre coil zone sits centred on it, with eight centimetres of clear margin at the crest so nothing is ever asked to charge on a curve. The ramp is the foot surface: longer than a foot, angled to hold one without sliding, and carrying no coils at all.

Every edge is radiused. Four centimetres at the crest, five where the ramp meets the front face, three at the back, and a quiet 1.5 at the floor. The five-centimetre radius is the one that matters — it's the edge a heel or an ankle crosses, and it's the reason the object reads as furniture rather than as a plinth with a lid.

How it holds a device

A grid of neodymium discs under the flat top pulls Qi2 and MagSafe-class devices into alignment and holds them there. The surface itself is tight-woven microfiber over a thin closed-cell layer — high friction, low compression set, no heel divots, and a predictable coil gap that stays inside Qi's tolerance whether the pad is empty or someone's sitting on the edge of it.

Foreign object detection runs continuously. Keys, coins, cutlery, an abandoned spoon: the pad sees a lossy load, idles that zone, and waits. It is not clever about it. It is simply unwilling to energise a coil it can't account for.

Feet

Sitting on a sofa with the angled padding nearest you, gives your feet a warm, supported surface. The ramp is long enough to take a whole foot, and the angle is shallow enough that a heel can rest on it without the toes dangling. The knit face is soft, and the viscoelastic layer underneath gives a little, so the foot is supported without being squashed. The ramp is not a massage surface, and it does not vibrate. It is simply a warm, comfortable surface for your feet to rest on while you charge your devices.

The ramp runs warm. A resistive film under the knit face holds 38–40 °C, thermistor- limited, on a twenty-minute auto-off. Warmth is the one thing a foot surface can honestly claim: cold toes are a perfusion problem, and conducted heat into unloaded skin is genuinely what helps.

Drawer

The base holds a device drawer, roughly thirty-four by fifty centimetres and twelve deep, on a coil of its own. Its front panel is flush and smoked-translucent, so the charge glow reads through it as a soft amber wash rather than a status light. It opens on the far face — reachable when you stand, invisible from the sofa.

The drawer is vented at the back. Qi waste heat in a closed box will throttle a phone, and a charging drawer that cooks its contents is worse than no charging drawer.

Light

Edge-lit under the lip, 2700 K, motion-triggered, spilling down onto the floor rather than up into the room. Charging state shows as a faint diffusion through the microfiber, not as an LED. Nothing on this object blinks.

Materials

  • Moulded shell — glass-fibre-reinforced polymer over a recycled aluminium spreader
  • Ramp face — looped knit over viscoelastic, zip-off
  • Top face — tight-woven microfiber over closed-cell, zip-off
  • Drawer front — smoked polycarbonate, flush, push-latch
